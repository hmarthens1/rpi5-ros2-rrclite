# LLM- and VLM-driven exploration, mapping and navigation for mobile robots (2024–2026)

Verification note: every arXiv ID below was checked against the arXiv API (export.arxiv.org) on 2026-10-17. The title, first-submission date and the author comment or journal field were confirmed. Venue claims come from that comment or journal field unless marked otherwise. Numbers marked "(abstract)" come from the arXiv abstract. Numbers marked "(full text)" come from reading the arXiv HTML version through a summarising fetch tool, so check them against the PDF before quoting them in print. Items marked UNVERIFIED were seen only in search snippets.

## 1. Semantic / LLM-guided frontier exploration: methods and gains over classical frontier exploration

### Takeaway
The standard 2024–2026 pattern is a classical geometric frontier map (from depth or 2D lidar) with each frontier scored by a VLM or LLM: a value map, commonsense scores, a scene-graph prompt, or a direct "pick a frontier" VLM query. A conventional planner then drives to the chosen frontier. On HM3D ObjectNav, zero-shot success went from about 39% (ESC, 2023) to about 52–58% (VLFM, InstructNav) and 65–73% in synchronous simulation (BeliefMapNav, HM3D-v1/v2). Head-to-head comparisons against plain nearest-frontier exploration are rare. The 2026 RTNav study shows that heavy VLM pipelines lose much of their advantage once inference latency counts against wall-clock time. Lightweight value-map methods (VLFM, L3MVN) hold up best.

### Cited Findings
**Background, pre-2024 but still baselines**
- ESC (Exploration with Soft Commonsense Constraints, arXiv 2301.13166, Jan 2023): uses LLM commonsense as soft constraints on frontier choice. The abstract reports SOTA zero-shot ObjectNav on MP3D/HM3D/RoboTHOR, including "288% relative Success Rate improvement than CoW on MP3D". VLFM's table lists ESC at HM3D 39.2 SR / 22.3 SPL and MP3D 28.7 / 14.2 — [arXiv 2301.13166](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13166); [VLFM full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.03275)
- L3MVN (arXiv 2304.05501, IROS 2023 per journal ref): an LLM picks the relevant frontier from a semantic map as the long-term goal. Real-robot experiment reported (abstract). It reaches 47.9 SR on HM3D-v1 and 58.9 on HM3D-v2 under synchronous evaluation, as reported in RTNav's re-evaluation — [arXiv 2304.05501](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.05501); [RTNav full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26496)
- SayNav (arXiv 2309.04077): builds a 3D scene graph incrementally as LLM input for high-level navigation plans. The abstract says it "outperforms an oracle based baseline with strong ground-truth assumptions by more than 8% in terms of success rate" — [arXiv 2309.04077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.04077)
- LFG, Language Frontier Guide (arXiv 2310.10103, Oct 2023): uses LLM "semantic guesswork" as a search heuristic over topological or metric maps. The abstract reports it beats uninformed exploration in real-world and simulated tests — [arXiv 2310.10103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10103)
- VLFM (arXiv 2312.03275, Dec 2023; widely cited as ICRA 2024, but the arXiv record has no venue field, so that venue is UNVERIFIED). It builds an occupancy map from depth to get frontiers and a BLIP-2 language-grounded value map from RGB, then picks the highest-value frontier. Full text: Gibson 84.0 SR / 52.2 SPL, HM3D 52.5 / 30.4, MP3D 36.4 / 17.5. These beat ESC, ZSON and CoW, and beat the trained PONI on Gibson and MP3D. Trained PIRLNav still has higher HM3D SR (64.1) but lower SPL (27.1). The paper has no plain frontier-only ablation; CoW, which explores the nearest frontier, is the closest comparison at 7.4 SR on MP3D — [VLFM full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.03275)
- VLFM real-world deployment: Boston Dynamics Spot. The gripper camera supplies RGB, body depth cameras supply the obstacle and frontier map, and ZoeDepth estimates depth for target waypoints. It uses BLIP-2, GroundingDINO and MobileSAM, all running in real time on a laptop with an RTX 4090 Mobile GPU (16 GB). No quantitative real-world success rate is reported — [VLFM full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.03275)

**2024–2026 methods**
- VoroNav (arXiv 2401.02695): a Reduced Voronoi Graph on a real-time semantic map, with the LLM choosing among exploration nodes. Gains over prior SOTA: +2.8% SR / +3.7% SPL on HM3D and +2.6% / +3.8% on HSSD (abstract) — [arXiv 2401.02695](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02695)
- OpenFMNav (arXiv 2402.10670, NAACL 2024 Findings): an LLM extracts candidate objects from a free-form instruction, and a VLM builds a "Versatile Semantic Score Map" for exploration. It beats baselines on HM3D, with real-robot demos (abstract). RTNav's re-evaluation gives 46.3 SR (HM3D-v1, synchronous) and 57.5 (HM3D-v2) — [arXiv 2402.10670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10670); [RTNav](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26496)
- InstructNav (arXiv 2406.04882; comment says "Submitted to CoRL 2024"; acceptance not verified): a Dynamic Chain-of-Navigation with GPT-4 plus multi-sourced value maps, and GPT-4V for an "intuition" value map. Full text: HM3D ObjectNav 58.0 SR / 20.9 SPL, with the ablation table giving 56 / 22.5, so the paper is internally inconsistent. The abstract says +10.48% over previous zero-shot SOTA on ObjectNav and +86.34% on demand-driven navigation (DDN). It is also the first zero-shot method on R2R-CE — [arXiv 2406.04882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04882); [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04882)
- InstructNav real robot (full text). The hardware is very close to the user's: TurtleBot 4, Orbbec Astra Pro Plus RGB-D, RPLIDAR-A1 on a Raspberry Pi 4B, SLAM Toolbox plus Nav2, and a ThinkPad laptop, with InstructNav itself running on a remote RTX 4090 workstation. No trial counts or success rates are reported, only qualitative scenes (offices, apartments, library, gallery) with no pre-built map — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04882)
- SG-Nav (arXiv 2410.08189, NeurIPS 2024): prompts an LLM with an online 3D scene graph. It beats prior zero-shot SOTA by more than 10% SR on MP3D, HM3D and RoboTHOR. The authors claim it is the first zero-shot method to beat supervised methods on MP3D (abstract) — [arXiv 2410.08189](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.08189)
- GAMap (arXiv 2410.23978): multi-scale geometric-part and affordance guidance. It reports SR/SPL gains on HM3D and Gibson (abstract). RTNav measures 48.8 / 61.3 SR (HM3D-v1/v2, synchronous) — [arXiv 2410.23978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23978); [RTNav](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26496)
- NavVLM (arXiv 2410.02787, "under review"): a training-free open-source VLM acts as the exploration "cognitive core" for open language goals. It reports SOTA SPL on MP3D, HM3D and Gibson, plus real-robot indoor validation (abstract). A search snippet says it reached 60% real-world success on out-of-detector-domain goals versus 0% for baselines without VLM guidance, on a RealMan robot. That figure is UNVERIFIED because it was not read in the paper — [arXiv 2410.02787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02787)
- VLMnav (arXiv 2411.05755, "End-to-End Navigation with Vision Language Models"): treats navigation as VLM question answering over annotated action arrows, using Gemini Flash. Full text: ObjectNav 50.4% SR / 0.210 SPL versus Prompt-Only 29.8% and PIVOT 24.6%. GOAT-Bench: 16.3% SR versus specialised Modular GOAT 24.9% and SenseAct-NN 29.5%. Depth is used for navigability; with Segformer floor segmentation instead SR is 47.2%, and with ZoeDepth 39.1%. There are no real-robot experiments, and performance collapses when simulator "sliding" is disabled (12.9% SR) — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05755)
- TopV-Nav (arXiv 2411.16425): an MLLM reasons directly over a top-view map for zero-shot ObjectNav, reported superior on MP3D/HM3D (abstract; no numbers given there) — [arXiv 2411.16425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16425)
- CogNav (arXiv 2412.10439): models the LLM's cognitive process as states such as exploring or identifying. It claims at least 14% relative SR improvement over SOTA on HM3D, MP3D and RoboTHOR (abstract) — [arXiv 2412.10439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10439)
- WMNav (arXiv 2503.02247, IROS 2025): treats a VLM as a world model that predicts the value of unseen regions. Gains: +3.2% SR / +3.2% SPL on HM3D and +13.5% SR / +1.1% SPL on MP3D (abstract) — [arXiv 2503.02247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02247)
- UniGoal (arXiv 2503.10630, CVPR 2025): universal zero-shot goal navigation over object, image and text goals using graph matching with an LLM — [arXiv 2503.10630](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10630)
- ApexNav (arXiv 2504.14478, RA-L Aug 2025): adaptive semantic versus geometric exploration with target-centric fusion. It beats SOTA on SR and SPL on HM3Dv1, HM3Dv2 and MP3D, plus real-world experiments (abstract) — [arXiv 2504.14478](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14478)
- BeliefMapNav (arXiv 2506.06487, May 2025): a 3D voxel belief map. It is the strongest synchronous method in RTNav's table at 65.5 SR on HM3D-v1 and 73.5 on HM3D-v2 — [arXiv 2506.06487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06487); [RTNav](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26496)
- FiLM-Nav (arXiv 2509.16445): fine-tunes a VLM to choose the next frontier from raw visual history. It claims SOTA SPL and SR on HM3D ObjectNav among open-vocabulary methods and SOTA SPL on HM3D-OVON (abstract). Its main results are in simulation — [arXiv 2509.16445](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.16445)
- "LLM as frontier selector" for pure exploration, not object search. Autonomous Frontier-Based Exploration with VLM Guidance (Aitha and Zakhor, arXiv 2605.23165, CVPR 2026 3D-LLM/VLA workshop) sends the map plus images of candidate frontiers to a VLM at decision points, while a conventional stack does control. It claims "up to 24%" better map coverage than "existing methods" across six simulated indoor environments. The abstract names neither the baselines nor the VLM, and reports no real robot — [arXiv 2605.23165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23165)
- LLM-MCoX (arXiv 2509.26324) handles multi-robot exploration. It ranks frontier samples by information gain minus travel cost and detects doorways by ray casting. The occupancy map is rendered as an image for GPT-4o, which returns waypoint sequences. Full text: about 22.7% faster exploration than greedy and Dynamic-Voronoi baselines with 6 robots in structured maps, and about 19.6% fewer steps than Mean-shift-Greedy in unstructured maps. With a natural-language hint it needs about 30.5% of Mean-shift-Greedy's steps. Real hardware was a Unitree Go2 and an X500 quadrotor running SLAM Toolbox plus Nav2 onboard, with a central computer over Wi-Fi and a 20 s replanning horizon. Real-world results are qualitative only — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26324v1)
- Explore until Confident (arXiv 2403.15941, RSS 2024): VLM-scored exploration for embodied question answering, using conformal prediction to calibrate when to stop exploring. It reports real-robot and simulation gains (abstract) — [arXiv 2403.15941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.15941)
- 3D-Mem (arXiv 2411.17735): "Frontier Snapshots" let a VLM weigh known memory against unexplored frontiers. It reports gains on three benchmarks (abstract) — [arXiv 2411.17735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17735)

**Latency reality check (most important 2026 finding)**
- RTNav (arXiv 2608.26496, Aug 2026) re-ran methods in real-time HM3D, where wall-clock inference counts toward the budget. Full text, SR as synchronous then real-time:
  - HM3D-v1: VLFM 52.6 then 51.3; L3MVN 47.9 then 47.7; OpenFMNav 46.3 then 32.9; Trihelper 52.7 then 43.7; GAMap 48.8 then 44.3; BeliefMapNav 65.5 then 27.3 (a drop of 38.2).
  - HM3D-v2: VLFM 64.0 then 63.2.
  - RTNav itself reaches 61.8 (v1) and 72.3 (v2) in real time.
  - The paper says "performance degrades consistently across all methods" — [RTNav full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26496)
- RTNav architecture: OWLv2 detection at about 15 Hz on an RTX A6000, and Qwen3.5-9B called on demand as an asynchronous server for synonym selection, frontier selection and target verification. Perception, mapping, planning and control run in separate threads. On a Jetson Thor it gets nearly the same SR and SPL (72.2 / 31.4). Real robot: Hello Robot Stretch 3 with a RealSense D435i head camera and a base 2D lidar for SLAM, running on a Jetson Thor over Wi-Fi, 16 targets in two building areas, with an FMM planner and PD controller replacing the simulated PointNav policy — [RTNav full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26496)

### Inferences
- The improvement over *classical* frontier exploration is almost never measured directly in ObjectNav papers. The comparison is usually against other learned or zero-shot methods. The best proxy is CoW (nearest frontier plus CLIP detection) at 7.4 SR on MP3D versus VLFM's 36.4. That suggests semantic frontier scoring roughly 3–5x's success on object search. It says nothing about full-coverage *mapping* time, which is what the user's greedy explorer optimises.
- For pure coverage exploration, the only LLM/VLM evidence found is moderate (up to 24% coverage in simulation; about 20–23% time savings for multi-robot teams). It comes with LLM latency of seconds per decision. For a single robot mapping a 47 m² home in 12.6 min, a classical, SLAM-aware explorer may matter more than an LLM. Region-based SLAM-aware exploration (arXiv 2504.10416) reports 10–15% shorter exploration duration with no LLM (see Q4).
- The InstructNav and RTNav real-robot setups (2D lidar SLAM plus RGB-D plus Nav2 or an FMM planner, with the VLM on a remote GPU) show that the user's architecture is a viable host. That means a Pi 5 running Hector/SLAM Toolbox and Nav2, an Aurora 930 RGB-D camera, and an off-board GPU laptop. VLFM-style value maps (BLIP-2 or a small VLM, about 16 GB of GPU) or RTNav-style async OWLv2 plus 4–9B VLM are the most latency-robust choices.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 paper found that reports *real-robot* ObjectNav success rates alongside the same method's simulation numbers. VLFM, InstructNav and LLM-MCoX give qualitative real-world results only.
- The baselines and VLM for arXiv 2605.23165 could not be confirmed from the abstract. ApexNav, SG-Nav, UniGoal and WMNav real-robot details were not read.
- Current HM3D leaderboard SOTA after Aug 2026 was not checked beyond RTNav's table.

## 2. Open-vocabulary 3D scene graphs and semantic maps for LLM planning: sensors, compute, embedded feasibility

### Takeaway
ConceptGraphs, HOV-SG, VLMaps and SayPlan need RGB-D plus accurate poses and a desktop- or laptop-class GPU. Most build maps offline or at about 0.1–0.5 Hz. Clio (MIT, Hydra-based) reaches about 3 fps on an RTX 4090 laptop. Genuine embedded operation (Jetson Orin NX or Thor) appears only in 2025–2026 work: FindAnything, SceneLM, RTNav on Jetson Thor, and a Go2 system. Nothing found runs on a Raspberry-Pi-class CPU, so off-board GPU is required for the user.

### Cited Findings
- VLMaps (arXiv 2210.05714, ICRA 2023, background): fuses pretrained visual-language features into a 3D reconstruction. Combined with an LLM it can turn commands into spatial goals such as "in between the sofa and TV" or "three meters to the right of the chair" — [arXiv 2210.05714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05714)
- SayPlan (arXiv 2307.06135, CoRL 2023 oral, background): the LLM does "semantic search" over a collapsed hierarchical 3D scene graph, with a classical path planner and iterative replanning. Tested on up to 3 floors, 36 rooms and 140 assets with a mobile manipulator (abstract) — [arXiv 2307.06135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06135)
- ConceptGraphs (arXiv 2309.16650): an open-vocabulary object graph that fuses 2D foundation-model outputs to 3D by multi-view association, with no 3D training data (abstract). Clio measured ConceptGraphs at 2.0–8.1 s per frame, versus about 0.3 s for Clio — [arXiv 2309.16650](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.16650); [Clio full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13696)
- HOV-SG (arXiv 2403.17846; ICRA 2024 per project materials, UNVERIFIED because the arXiv record has no venue field). It builds a floor → room → object hierarchy with open-vocabulary features and a cross-floor Voronoi graph, and claims a 75% smaller representation than dense open-vocabulary maps (abstract). Full text: on 8 HM3DSem scenes it uses 1493 MB, versus ConceptGraphs 1638 MB and VLMaps 6068 MB.
  - Real hardware: Spot with an Azure Kinect RGB-D camera and a 3D lidar (FAST-LIO2 plus loop closure). The GPU is not specified.
  - Real results in a two-storey office: object retrieval 70.7% (29/41), object navigation 56.1% (23/41), room navigation 55.6% (5/9).
  - The authors state that construction is "time-consuming, rendering the method unsuitable for real-time mapping", and that it assumes a static scene — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17846)
- Clio (arXiv 2404.13696): task-driven open-set 3D scene graphs. An Information-Bottleneck method clusters primitives to match the granularity of the given tasks. It is built on Hydra and uses a Hydra-style GVD places layer.
  - Sensors: Spot front RGB-D cameras plus odometry, with a RealSense D455 for datasets.
  - Compute: laptop with an i9-13950HX and RTX 4090 Laptop GPU, off-board over Wi-Fi in trials; the authors say the video shows onboard compute.
  - Models: FastSAM and CLIP ViT-L/14. Speed is about 0.26–0.30 s per frame, roughly 3 fps.
  - Spot grasping: 57% grasp success (71% excluding grasp-mechanics failures) — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13696)
- FindAnything (arXiv 2504.08603): object-centric open-vocabulary volumetric submaps using eSAM segments. The abstract claims it is "substantially faster and more memory-efficient", deployable on resource-constrained devices such as MAVs. A secondary source says it runs on a Jetson Orin NX at about 3 Hz (UNVERIFIED in the paper) — [arXiv 2504.08603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08603)
- Open-Architecture End-to-End System for Real-World Autonomous Robot Navigation (arXiv 2410.06239): Unitree Go2, onboard components over ROS 2, onboard localisation and mapping fused with open-vocabulary semantics into hierarchical scene graphs, and an LLM planner. It reports "over 88% task success" zero-shot across indoor environments (abstract). A search snippet says it pairs 2D-lidar SLAM with optimised open-vocabulary segmentation on Jetson compute; that detail is UNVERIFIED — [arXiv 2410.06239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06239)
- SceneLM (arXiv 2609.21400, Sep 2026): a single VLM keeps the scene map as a *structured text list of objects*. The map is 6–12x more compact than full pipelines, with competitive retrieval and localisation, and it runs online "on an edge device" on a quadruped (abstract) — [arXiv 2609.21400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21400)
- OVIP-SG (arXiv 2608.17633, Aug 2026): instance-preserving open-vocabulary scene graphs for small objects. On Replica it is +6.31 mAcc and +5.15 F-mIoU over ConceptGraphs. It cuts the search area to 21.8% of the floor and reaches 0.773 balanced accuracy at deciding whether an object is absent (abstract) — [arXiv 2608.17633](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17633)
- DAAAM (arXiv 2512.00565, Nov 2025): a real-time hierarchical 4D scene graph with Describe-Anything captions, queried by a tool-calling agent. It reports +53.6% question accuracy on OC-NaVQA (abstract) — [arXiv 2512.00565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.00565)
- ReMEmbR (arXiv 2409.13682, NVIDIA): retrieval-augmented long-horizon spatio-temporal robot memory for "where/when" queries, with the NaVQA dataset and robot deployment (abstract) — [arXiv 2409.13682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.13682)
- Embodied-RAG (arXiv 2409.18313): hierarchical non-parametric memory that handles more than 250 explanation and navigation queries over kilometre-scale environments (abstract) — [arXiv 2409.18313](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18313)
- Survey: "3D Scene Graphs: Open Challenges and Future Directions" (arXiv 2606.19383, invited for the Annual Review of Control, Robotics, and Autonomous Systems, Vol. 10). It says the field is fragmented in formulation and evaluation — [arXiv 2606.19383](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19383)

### Inferences
- For a 2D lidar plus RGB-D robot, the practical pattern is to keep the 2D occupancy SLAM (Hector or SLAM Toolbox) for geometry. Poses plus Aurora 930 RGB-D frames are then streamed to an off-board laptop GPU that builds an object-level open-vocabulary layer, ConceptGraphs/Clio-style or text-list SceneLM-style, anchored to the 2D map. Clio's 0.3 s per frame on a laptop RTX 4090 sets the realistic bar. ConceptGraphs-style pipelines (2–8 s per frame) are fine for a mapping-then-query workflow but not for online exploration decisions.
- HOV-SG's room layer would directly support "go to the kitchen". However, its real-world room success was only 55.6%, and it is offline and needs a 3D lidar plus Kinect.

### Gaps
- ConceptGraphs' own GPU and memory requirements were not read from the paper; the per-frame times come from Clio's benchmark.
- Jetson numbers for FindAnything and the Go2 system (2410.06239) are unverified. No work found runs any of these on a Raspberry Pi 5.

## 3. Language-conditioned goals in Nav2 / real robots; zero-shot ObjectNav sim-to-real

### Takeaway
Real-robot language navigation is now common, but evidence is mostly qualitative or comes from small trial counts. The strongest quantitative real-world numbers come from systems with a mapping or exploration phase followed by queries: GOAT at 83% over 675 goals, OK-Robot at 58.5% pick-and-drop across 10 homes, the Go2 scene-graph system at over 88%, and HOV-SG at 56% object navigation. Direct "language → VLM grounding → RGB-D 3D point → Nav2 goal" pipelines on ROS 2 exist and match the user's stack, including a 2026 paper on TurtleBot3 and Go2.

### Cited Findings
- From Language to Navigation Goals (arXiv 2607.13624, Jul 2026): modular ROS 2 components. A VLM identifies the referenced object (e.g., "go to the mail box"), its 3D position is estimated from RGB-D, and a goal is sent to **Nav2**. It handles direct and contextual requests and gives natural-language feedback. Tested in simulation and the real world on a TurtleBot3 Waffle and a Unitree Go2 with RealSense. The abstract gives no success numbers, and code is promised on acceptance — [arXiv 2607.13624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13624)
- InstructNav real-robot setup: TurtleBot 4, RPLIDAR-A1 on a Pi 4B, Astra RGB-D, SLAM Toolbox plus Nav2, with the LLM/VLM on a remote RTX 4090. This is the closest published analogue to the user's robot (see Q1) — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04882)
- GOAT: GO to Any Thing (arXiv 2311.06430, Nov 2023): multimodal goals (category, image, language) with lifelong memory. Over 90 hours in 9 homes with 675 goals it gets 83% success, +32% absolute over prior methods and ablations, rising from 60% on the first goal to 90% after exploration (abstract) — [arXiv 2311.06430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06430)
- OK-Robot (arXiv 2401.12202): open-knowledge navigation plus manipulation in 10 real homes. It gets 58.5% pick-and-drop success, about 1.8x prior work, and 82% in uncluttered homes. Its main lesson is that "nuanced details" of integration dominate (abstract) — [arXiv 2401.12202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12202)
- Mobility VLA (arXiv 2407.07775, Google): a long-context VLM finds the goal frame in a tour video, and a topological graph handles low-level actions. Tested in an 836 m² real environment with "high end-to-end success" on multimodal instructions such as "Where should I return this?" (abstract) — [arXiv 2407.07775](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07775)
- Open-architecture Go2 system (arXiv 2410.06239): ROS 2, open-vocabulary hierarchical scene graphs and an LLM planner, with over 88% zero-shot real-world task success (abstract) — [arXiv 2410.06239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06239)
- NaVid (arXiv 2402.15852, RSS 2024): a video-based VLM that uses only monocular RGB, "naturally gets rid of the problems introduced by odometer noises". Trained on 510k navigation samples plus 763k web samples, with sim-to-real transfer (abstract) — [arXiv 2402.15852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15852)
- Uni-NaVid (arXiv 2412.06224): a video VLA unifying navigation tasks, trained on 3.6M samples, with real-world experiments (abstract) — [arXiv 2412.06224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06224)
- NaVILA (arXiv 2412.04453): a VLA outputs mid-level language actions such as "moving forward 75cm" for a legged RL locomotion policy, with real-robot experiments (abstract) — [arXiv 2412.04453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04453)
- ROS-level LLM agents. ROSA, from NASA JPL (arXiv 2410.06472, IEEE Aerospace 2025), is a natural-language agent for ROS1/ROS2 with parameter validation and constraint enforcement, shown on three robots including in the JPL Mars Yard. ROS-LLM (arXiv 2406.19741, Nature Machine Intelligence 8, 313–325, 2026) turns LLM output into ROS actions, behaviour trees or state machines, with reflection from human and environment feedback — [arXiv 2410.06472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06472); [arXiv 2406.19741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19741)
- Sim-to-real issues. RTNav shows that synchronous-simulator results overstate real-time performance (see Q1). T-DRN (arXiv 2607.15642) identifies narrow camera FoV, which loses the target during rotation, and object-appearance variation as key sim-to-real gaps, and validates on a physical wheeled robot (abstract). VLMnav's performance collapses without simulator collision "sliding" (12.9% versus 50.4% SR) — [arXiv 2607.15642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15642); [VLMnav full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05755)
- Fast-SmartWay (arXiv 2511.00933): zero-shot VLN using only three frontal RGB-D images instead of panoramas, which cuts per-step latency. Tested in simulation and on a real robot (abstract) — [arXiv 2511.00933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00933)
- UNVERIFIED (search snippets only):
  - A Sensors (MDPI) paper, doi 10.3390/s26020608, "Latency-Aware Benchmarking of LLMs for Natural-Language Robot Navigation in ROS 2", reportedly ran over 900 Nav2 simulation trials — [Sensors](https://doi.org/10.3390/s26020608)
  - A Hackster project reportedly ran Qwen3-VL-4B on a Qualcomm IQ-9075 sending Nav2 goals in Gazebo — [Hackster](https://www.hackster.io/naveenbskumar/vlm-driven-ros-2-navigation-on-gazebo-via-dragonwing-iq-9075-add4b8)

### Inferences
- The lowest-risk path for "go to the kitchen" on the user's robot is a mapping-then-query design. Explore with the existing frontier explorer while logging RGB-D keyframes with SLAM poses. Off-board, build an object and room layer. At query time an LLM resolves the phrase to a room or object node, and a reachable pose on the 2D costmap is sent as a Nav2 `NavigateToPose` goal. This mirrors 2607.13624, InstructNav and GOAT. GOAT's 60% → 90% gain after exploration suggests the memory or map is the main driver of success.
- Real-time goal search during exploration should use asynchronous VLM calls (RTNav) so Nav2 never waits on the model.

### Gaps
- No standardised real-robot ObjectNav benchmark exists, and trial counts in most 2024–2026 real-robot sections are small or missing.
- Success numbers for 2607.13624 were not in the abstract, and the full text was not read.

## 4. LLMs/agents for SLAM itself, diagnosing robot/SLAM failures, and active SLAM / loop-closure-aware exploration

### Takeaway
This is the thinnest area. LLMs enter SLAM mainly as priors: object size and orientation factors, or scene-graph layers. LLM-based failure diagnosis exists for logs, tickets and manipulation actions, but **no paper was found in which an LLM or VLM detects odometry sign errors, calibration faults or SLAM map corruption** on a mobile robot. Active-SLAM and loop-closure-aware exploration remains classical, with one 2024 preprint linking active loop closing to LLM-guided ObjectNav. The user's lidar-only check that found inverted motor signs is therefore not covered by published LLM work.

### Cited Findings
- Real-Time Indoor Object SLAM with LLM-Enhanced Priors (arXiv 2509.21602): an LLM supplies commonsense object size and orientation as prior factors in graph SLAM. It improves mapping accuracy by 36.8% over the latest baseline on TUM RGB-D and 3RScan, in real time (abstract) — [arXiv 2509.21602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21602)
- Diagnosing Robotics Systems Issues with LLMs (arXiv 2410.09084, ABB-affiliated per a search snippet). It uses SYSDIAGBENCH, a proprietary set of more than 2500 issues, for root-cause analysis from logs. A QLoRA-fine-tuned 7B model beats GPT-4 on diagnostic accuracy and gets approval ratings from human experts similar to the reference labels (abstract) — [arXiv 2410.09084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09084)
- RAIDER (arXiv 2503.17703): a tool-equipped LLM agent with a "Ground, Ask&Answer, Issue" procedure for action-issue detection, explanation and recovery. Evaluated in a simulated household plus one real assistive task (abstract) — [arXiv 2503.17703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17703)
- Background: REFLECT (CoRL 2023, PMLR v229) summarises multisensory robot experience hierarchically so an LLM can explain and correct failures — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v229/liu23g/liu23g.pdf)
- VLM failure monitors for manipulation and task execution, such as Guardian (arXiv 2512.01946), exist. Their scope is planning and execution errors, not localisation. Guardian's details were seen in a search snippet only, so the ID is unverified beyond the search result — [arXiv 2512.01946](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01946)
- Industry: Roboto Agents (Open Robotics Discourse announcement) claims agentic triage and root-cause analysis over rosbag and MCAP logs. This is a vendor claim and was not evaluated — [Discourse](https://discourse.openrobotics.org/t/roboto-agents-agentic-triage-root-cause-analysis-and-data-curation-for-rosbags-and-mcap/57438)
- Non-LLM SLAM health checks, the closest to the user's case:
  - SENTINEL (arXiv 2606.04853, ICRA 2026 Uncertainty in Open-World Robotics workshop) gives low-cost 2D lidar a per-scan reliability score in [0,1] by combining scan geometry statistics with lidar-versus-RGB-D depth consistency. Below a threshold it rejects scans and falls back to calibrated wheel odometry to prevent "silent SLAM corruption". It was validated on a real skid-steer robot with an RPLidar A2M12 and RealSense D435i against glass, mirror and shiny surfaces.
  - "Robot Vitals" (arXiv 2207.01684, background) uses indicators such as the divergence between wheel odometry and EKF or visual odometry to quantify runtime degradation — [arXiv 2606.04853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04853); [arXiv 2207.01684](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01684)
- Counterfactual fault diagnosis for perception (arXiv 2509.18460) is not LLM-based. It uses analytical rather than physical redundancy, and active fault detection and isolation via MCTS to choose informative control inputs — [arXiv 2509.18460](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18460)
- Active SLAM and loop-closure-aware exploration:
  - Region Based SLAM-Aware Exploration (arXiv 2504.10416), no LLM: explore and stabilise one region at a time, with keyframe marginalisation and checkpoints. In real homes and offices it cuts keyframes by 85%, submaps by 32–50%, pose-graph optimisation time by 78–80% and exploration duration by 10–15%.
  - ON as ALC / ALCON (arXiv 2412.11523, draft): proposes using frontier-guided, data-driven and LLM-guided ObjectNav planners for long-distance active loop closing.
  - Multi-robot lidar SLAM in tunnels (arXiv 2507.21553) found loop-detection false positives to be a dominant failure source — [arXiv 2504.10416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10416); [arXiv 2412.11523](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.11523); [arXiv 2507.21553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21553)

### Inferences
- An "LLM diagnostician" for the user's robot would be novel. A plausible design follows RAIDER and SYSDIAGBENCH: an LLM agent with tools that read `/odom`, `/scan`, `/tf` and the map. The tools would compute discrepancy checks such as scan-matching yaw versus commanded or odometry yaw (which would expose inverted motor signs), lidar-versus-RGB-D depth consistency (SENTINEL) and loop-closure residuals. The LLM explains the result; the numeric checks do the detection. The literature does not support having the LLM perceive such faults directly from raw data.
- SLAM-aware exploration (region stabilisation, active loop closing) is likely a better upgrade for the user's Hector SLAM plus greedy frontier mapping than LLM frontier scoring. Hector SLAM has no loop closure, so switching to SLAM Toolbox would be a prerequisite.

### Gaps
- No published LLM or VLM method found for detecting odometry sign or calibration errors, judging 2D map quality, or explaining map artifacts. A targeted search returned nothing relevant.
- No LLM-driven active SLAM method with quantitative loop-closure or uncertainty results was found. ALCON is a draft without verified numbers.

## 5. Benchmarks (HM3D ObjectNav, GOAT-Bench, HM3D-OVON) and real-robot comparison

### Takeaway
HM3D ObjectNav (v1 and v2), MP3D and Gibson remain the core zero-shot benchmarks, alongside HM3D-OVON (379 open-vocabulary categories) and GOAT-Bench (multimodal lifelong goals). Zero-shot HM3D-v2 SR is about 64–73% in synchronous simulation. GOAT-Bench is far from solved at about 16–30% SR. Real-robot reporting is inconsistent, and the 2026 real-time evaluations show that simulation numbers overstate deployable performance, especially for heavy VLM pipelines.

### Cited Findings
- HM3D-OVON (arXiv 2409.14296): more than 15k annotated instances in 379 categories, against 6–20 categories in earlier ObjectNav. Goals are free-form text at test time. The authors report that agents trained on it are more robust to localisation and actuation noise than the prior SOTA (abstract) — [arXiv 2409.14296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14296)
- GOAT-Bench (arXiv 2404.06609): 5–10 sequential goals per episode, given as category, language or image. Success means stopping within 1 m within 500 actions. Full text: val-unseen SR/SPL is Modular GOAT 24.9 / 17.2, SenseAct-NN Skill Chain 29.5 / 11.3, Modular CoW 16.1 / 10.4, and GOAT with ground-truth semantics 54.3 / 41.0. Removing memory cuts Modular GOAT's SPL from 17.6 to 9.4. Simulation only, using the Stretch embodiment. The paper's dataset totals are internally inconsistent (181 vs 145+36 scenes; 680k vs about 725k episodes) — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.06609)
- VLMnav on GOAT-Bench: 16.3% SR, below the specialised baselines — [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.05755)
- Zero-shot HM3D values under synchronous evaluation: VLFM 52.5 SR (own paper, HM3D) and 52.6 / 64.0 on v1/v2 as re-measured by RTNav; InstructNav 58.0; BeliefMapNav 65.5 / 73.5. Real-time evaluation: RTNav 61.8 / 72.3, and RTNav on OVON 44.6 versus VLFM 38.2 — [VLFM](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.03275); [InstructNav](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04882); [RTNav](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.26496)
- Real-robot reference points:
  - GOAT: 83% across 675 goals in 9 homes.
  - OK-Robot: 58.5% in 10 homes.
  - HOV-SG: 56.1% object navigation (23/41).
  - Go2 scene-graph system: over 88%.
  - VLFM, InstructNav, LLM-MCoX, OpenFMNav, ApexNav: real-world demos with no or limited quantitative success, based on the abstracts and full texts read.
  - Sources: [GOAT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.06430); [OK-Robot](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12202); [HOV-SG](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.17846); [2410.06239](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06239)

### Inferences
- Real-world numbers are not comparable to HM3D numbers. Real tests often have a pre-explored map (GOAT, OK-Robot), use small goal sets, or lack trial counts. The RTNav real-time protocol, which measures success weighted by completion time, is the most deployment-relevant simulation metric for a slow-compute robot.
- For the user, a home-scale self-benchmark (e.g., N objects × M start poses in the 47 m² home, reporting SR, path length and wall-clock time against the greedy frontier baseline) would add more evidence than further HM3D comparisons.

### Gaps
- An up-to-date official HM3D ObjectNav or OVON challenge leaderboard for 2025–2026 was not consulted.
- No benchmark found evaluates exploration *coverage* with 2D lidar on low-cost robots with LLM guidance.
